Use case: scientific-educational.
Create a new high-quality academic poster illustration for SLICE, a compact reader of a language model's internal states. The audience is a novice. This must be a VISUAL explanation, not a flowchart of text-filled rectangular boxes. Landscape approximately 2:1, white background, crisp publication-quality diagram, light isometric paper illustration, elegant navy, pale blue and teal, tiny restrained gold accents, no dark background. Strong readable hierarchy, ample whitespace, minimal words, ALL TEXT UPPERCASE.

Read the following as precise scientific constraints:
A frozen transformer processes a QUESTION and OPTIONAL PARTIAL ANSWER. Signals from several of its internal layers feed a learned frozen compressor which outputs 768 numeric features. A small linear Ridge readout produces a score estimating whether the eventual final answer will be correct. No completed answer or ground-truth reference is input into that prediction. No RL or training loop in this overview. The compressor uses ordinary hidden activations, NOT J-lens, and is nonlinear though the final readout is linear.

Composition: one flowing left-to-right illustration in a single clean scene:
1. Far left, two small pieces of readable text, like words on paper, labeled "QUESTION" and "PARTIAL ANSWER". Use actual example "9 AVOCADOS, 3 PER SERVING?" and partial answer "DIVIDE 9 BY..." with trailing ellipsis, deliberately no final answer. A small text strip/tokens carries these words via a clear arrow into the transformer.
2. Dominant center-left object: a handsome stack of thin, translucent paper sheets, visibly separated, in gentle isometric perspective, a literal transformer-layer stack. Suggest many layers with a vertical ellipsis; do NOT attempt all 28 literal sheets. Label below "FROZEN LANGUAGE MODEL" and a smaller "LAYERS SHOWN SCHEMATICALLY". A small lock icon signifies frozen weights, not a security shield. Show dots/short numeric marks or subtle horizontal word-like marks ON the sheets representing activations; no invented chain-of-thought text. Five sheets at separated depths carry teal highlights. Their captured internal states leave from the side, through five clearly visible delicate teal streams, converging to the next object. The page stack must remain easy to recognize as layers, not books/files of external documents.
3. Center-right: five small parallel ribbons of colored numeric dots converge into a clean compact taper/funnel-like projection, labeled "SLICE". At the wide entrance use the short label "INTERNAL STATES". At its narrow exit, a short neatly arranged 3-by-8 array of colored circles/rectangles represents a numeric feature vector (some pale or empty), with the label "768 FEATURES" beneath. This is schematic; do not draw 768 dots. Labels do not claim that those features are words or interpretable concepts.
4. Far right: a very small simple three-input weighted-sum symbol (three dots with connecting edges to one output) labeled "LINEAR READOUT", then a simple outlined semicircular confidence dial labeled "CORRECTNESS SCORE". The dial has endpoints "LOW" and "HIGH", neutral dark teal needle angled somewhat right, NO numerical probability and NO checkmark guaranteeing correctness. Under it a short two-line caption "WILL THE FINAL ANSWER / BE CORRECT?".
5. Top title only: "SLICE: READING SIGNS OF CORRECTNESS".
6. One short footer: "ONLY THE QUESTION AND AVAILABLE PREFIX ARE READ."

Avoid: text paragraphs, specification panels, UI cards, dense training details, mathematical equations, arrows returning backwards, robot or human mascots, brains, evaluator/Math-Verify boxes, final answer 3, invented percentages, speed claims, guarantees of correctness, claims about knowing or understanding, decorative clutter, spelling errors, overlapping labels. The transformer paper stack and the clearly visible flow from its sheets into the small reader should dominate the image. Beautiful enough for a serious academic poster, intuitive enough to explain without reading a paragraph.
